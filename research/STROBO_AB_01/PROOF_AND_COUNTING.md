# STROBO-AB-01: algebraic proof, internal only
Let G be a finite abelian group with G[2]={0,h}, h!=0, Q=G/<h> and pi:G->Q. For each three-point support S define f_S=sum_[s in S][s] in Z[G]; its square has coefficients counting ordered sums (diagonals included).
In F_2[G], f_S^2=sum_[s in S][2s]. Thus equal squares imply same fibre occupancy parities for the doubling fibres pi^{-1}(q), each of size two. Three points entail exactly two profiles: three singly occupied fibres, or one singly occupied fibre plus one doubly occupied fibre. For the first, all candidate T arise from independent h toggles. Modulo the global toggle, a nontrivial switch of one element a changes square by 2[a]([h]-[0])([b]+[c]); equality would force c=b+h, violating distinct fibres.
For the second, write S={a,a+h,s} and T={c,c+h,t}, pi(s)=pi(t)=b, alpha=pi(a),gamma=pi(c) with alpha,gamma!=b. The additive (not multiplicative) map L:Z[Q]->Z[G], [q] |-> [g]+[g+h], is independent of lift and injective. The squared convolution equals [2s]+2L([2alpha]+[alpha+b]). Consequently equality is equivalent to the two-term multiset identity {2alpha,alpha+b}={2gamma,gamma+b}. The direct pairing gives gamma=alpha and S or S+h. The crossed pairing gives gamma=2alpha-b, 3(alpha-b)=0 and alpha-b!=0, an exact order-three element x. Hence exceptional pairs are S=pi^{-1}(b+x) union {s}, T=pi^{-1}(b+2x) union {t}, with s,t in pi^{-1}(b), up to exchanging supports. All exceptional pairs satisfy equality conversely.
Translation exclusion: a shift mapping S to T must preserve the unique singleton fibre b, so it projects to 0 in Q and cannot change the double fibre from b+x to b+2x.
Counting: For each b in Q and order-3 subgroup K<=Q, choose one nonzero x in K to orient the unordered pair; four singleton-lift choices give 4|Q|r_3=2|G|r_3 exception pairs (with zero). Intrinsic singleton b and line K prevent double counting. Excluding zero removes 3 pairs at b=0, 4 at b=x and 4 at b=2x, hence r_3(2|G|-11).
Half-translated pairs without zero = C(|G|-2,3)/2. Fibre distribution for q=|Q| and r=r_3:
with zero F4=qr, F2=C(|G|,3)/2-2qr, F1=0;
without zero F4=(q-3)r, F2=C(|G|-2,3)/2-2qr+7r, F1=C(|G|-2,2)-2r.
For Q[3] of dimension t over F3, r_3=(3^t-1)/2. Odd-order G: doubling is bijective, so S=T.
