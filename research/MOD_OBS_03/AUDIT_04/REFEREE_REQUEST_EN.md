# Draft referee request -- NOT SENT

Subject: Request for a critical check of a small cyclic additive-representation result

Dear Professor,

I am seeking a genuinely adversarial assessment of a finite-group calculation, prepared with AI assistance and still entirely unreviewed externally. I would especially welcome a counterexample or a reference to an existing equivalent theorem.

For an oriented Cayley digraph on Z/nZ generated by a three-element set S, let K_S(r) count ORDERED solutions s+t=r in S x S. We study when K_S=K_T and how many additional scalar cubic observations suffice to reconstruct S or decide only its strong connectivity.

Our most recent internal proof shows that K_S alone determines strong connectivity if n is odd or divisible by four; when n=2h with h odd >=5, S={2,4,6} and T=S+h have the same K but opposite connectivity. At n=22, S={1,3,13} and T={2,12,14} also have equal cubic traces (both zero), showing that a useful n=18 trace test does not generalize. An explicit exceptional four-support family has two cubic coefficients with four distinct ordered pairs of responses.

The precise claims, elementary proofs, integer checker, finite observations and publication boundaries are assembled in the linked GitHub research branch and Drive evidence pack. We do not assert originality or mathematical priority. Could you check (i) the parity proof, (ii) the exceptional-fibre classification and its zero-exclusion count, (iii) the probe-cost assumptions, and (iv) whether the result already exists under another representation-function formulation?

No endorsement is implied if you cannot review the note. Critical feedback, including a single incorrect hypothesis or missing prior citation, would be invaluable.

With thanks,
Alexandre Couret
